# ASD-STE100: requirements and fit with this repo

Question: what does ASD-STE100 require in its current issue, and where does it conflict with `.agents/writing-language.md`?

## Sources and method

Primary sources only. All were read directly on 2026-10-28.

- **Spec:** ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English, Issue 9, 2025-01-15 (PDF). Fetched from `https://www.asd-ste100.org/assets/files/ASD-STE100_ISSUE9.pdf` and read as extracted text. Cited below as "Issue 9".
- **Site pages:** `https://www.asd-ste100.org/STE_downloads.html`, `STE_faq.html`, `software.html`. Cited by page name.
- **Not read:** the STEMG white paper "Simplified Technical English and Artificial Intelligence" (linked from the downloads page) and the training pages. Anything that might live only there is marked unverified.

Issue 9 is the current issue. Its Highlights page says it "fully replaces all other issues". The downloads page labels it "Issue 9, January 2025". Part 1 has 9 sections and 53 rules (Issue 9, General introduction, "Guide to the writing rules").

## Scope limits the spec states about itself

- STE is for technical documentation. It cannot be used alone. It works together with other style guides (Issue 9, General introduction, "Can STE be used alone?").
- STE has no rules for abbreviations (General introduction, "Does STE give rules for abbreviations?"). Rule 8.3 only permits parentheses to include abbreviations.
- STE has no rules for text formatting or units of measurement (General introduction). Headings, numbering and typeface are out of scope.
- The spec offers "recommendations" with no legal force (Disclaimer of liability).

## Writing rules (Part 1, Issue 9)

### Section 1, Words

- **1.1** Use only words that are approved in the dictionary, technical nouns, or technical verbs.
- **1.2** Use an approved word only as the part of speech the dictionary gives.
- **1.3** Use an approved word only with its approved meaning. Example: "fall" means "move down by gravity", not "decrease" (General introduction).
- **1.4** Use only the approved forms of verbs and adjectives.
- **1.5** A technical noun is a noun term for a specified concept in a subject field. It is not in the dictionary. Rule 1.5 gives 22 categories, with examples, that a term must fit (for example official parts information, materials, tools).
- **1.6** A word that is not approved is allowed only as a technical noun or part of one. Example: "base" is not approved for a surface (use "bottom"), but "the base of the triangle" is allowed.
- **1.7** Do not use a technical noun as a verb. Non-STE: "Oil the steel surfaces." STE: "Apply oil to the steel surfaces."
- **1.8** Use the technical noun your company, industry or field already approves.
- **1.9** If none exists, pick one that is short (not more than three words) and easy to understand.
- **1.10** No regional, slang or jargon words as technical nouns.
- **1.11** One technical noun per item. Do not alternate "servo control unit", "actuator" and "control unit" for the same thing.
- **1.12** Technical verbs are allowed if they fit a technical verb category.
- **1.13** Do not use a technical verb as a noun.
- **1.14** American English spelling.

### Section 2, Multi-word nouns (noun clusters)

- **2.1** A multi-word noun has at most three words. Break longer ones with prepositions. Non-STE: "Runway light connection resistance calibration" (5 words). STE: "Calibration of the resistance of the runway light connection."
- **2.2** A technical noun longer than three words is written in full. Then either give a shorter form or join words with hyphens as one unit. Example: write "ramp service door safety connector pin" once, then use "safety connector pin".

### Section 3, Verbs

- **3.1** Use only verb forms the dictionary gives.
- **3.2** Allowed: infinitive, imperative, simple present, simple past, simple future, past participle used as an adjective. Not allowed: present perfect, past perfect, progressive tenses, and other complex constructions.
- **3.3** Use a past participle as an adjective only before a noun or after "be", "become" or "stay", and only if the dictionary lists it.
- **3.4** No auxiliary verbs that build complex verb constructions. Non-STE: "The volume control can be adjusted." STE: "You can adjust the volume control."
- **3.5** Use the "-ing" form only as a technical noun or as a modifier inside a technical noun. A few dictionary words end in "-ing" (for example "during", and "opening" as a noun).
- **3.6** Use the active voice. In descriptive writing, the passive is allowed only when the agent is unknown. Example: "During transmission, the data was corrupted."
- **3.7** Use an approved verb for an action, not a noun. "The ohmmeter shows 450 ohms", not "gives an indication of 450 ohms".

### Section 4, Sentences (both writing types)

- **4.1** Short, clear sentences. In descriptive text each sentence has one topic.
- **4.2** Do not drop words and do not use contractions ("don't", "isn't").
- **4.3** Use a vertical list for complex text. The sentence before the list ends with a colon. Each item starts with an uppercase letter. An item that is a full sentence ends with a period. The last item always ends with a period. No commas or semicolons at item ends.
- **4.4** Use connecting words and phrases ("and", "but", "then", "thus", "as a result") to link related sentences.
- **4.5** Use an article or demonstrative ("the", "a", "this", "these") before a noun when applicable. Do not use articles in general statements ("Solvents can cause damage to paint").

### Section 5, Procedures

- **5.1** Maximum 20 words per sentence. Safety instructions obey this too.
- **5.2** One instruction per sentence, unless two actions happen at the same time. Show sequence with numbers or letters. There is no limit on step count.
- **5.3** Instructions use the imperative.
- **5.4** If the reader must know a condition first, put it first, then a comma, then the command. "When the surface is dry, apply the primer."
- **5.5** Notes give information only, never instructions, requirements or limits. A note sentence has at most 25 words. No imperative in a note.

### Section 6, Descriptive writing

- **6.1** Give information gradually, one subject per sentence.
- **6.2** Use key words and key phrases to give a logical structure.
- **6.3** Maximum 25 words per sentence.
- **6.4** Use paragraphs for related information.
- **6.5** One topic per paragraph.
- **6.6** Maximum 6 sentences per paragraph.
- Descriptive writing never uses the imperative (Section 6, "Content structure").

### Section 7, Safety instructions

- **7.1** Use a level word ("warning", "caution") to show the risk level.
- **7.2** Start with a clear command or condition.
- **7.3** Give the reason (the risk or result).

### Section 8, Punctuation and word count

- **8.1** All standard punctuation except the semicolon.
- **8.2** Hyphens join directly related words.
- **8.3** Parentheses are allowed for references, step labels, abbreviations, plural forms, explanations and alternatives.
- **8.4** In a vertical list, a colon ends a sentence for word counting.
- **8.5** Text in parentheses counts as one word.
- **8.6** One word each: numbers, numbers with units, abbreviations, alphanumeric identifiers, quoted text, titles, headings, labels, proper nouns.
- **8.7** A hyphenated word counts as one word.

### Section 9, Writing practices

- **9.1** If a word-for-word swap fails, rebuild the sentence.
- **9.2** Use each approved word correctly (restricted meanings).
- **9.3** Do not make phrasal verbs by putting two words together.
- **9.4** Keep terminology and wording consistent.
- General recommendations GR-1 ("that") and GR-2 ("with") are not rules.

## Approved dictionary (Part 2, Issue 9)

- It lists 875 approved words and 1274 words that are not approved (Part 2, Introduction, "General"). The FAQ says "approximately 900". Treat 875 as the exact figure.
- Each non-approved word has one or more approved alternatives and an example.
- Entry format: four columns. Word and part of speech, approved meaning or alternatives, STE example, non-STE example. UPPERCASE means approved, lowercase means not approved.
- Each approved word is approved with one part of speech and mostly one meaning. Example: "check" is a noun only ("do a check"). Of a set of synonyms, one word stays ("start", not "begin").
- Nouns appear singular. Plurals of countable nouns are allowed unless a help note says otherwise.
- Technical nouns (TN) and technical verbs (TV) are outside the dictionary. They come from the author's own glossary under rules 1.5 and 1.12. This is how a developer docs set can use terms such as "callback" without the dictionary listing them. Whether a given developer term fits one of the 22 categories was not checked.
- Spelling follows Merriam-Webster (American English).

## Free to use?

- **Reading and copying:** the spec is free of charge to all writers and users. Source: Issue 9, General introduction, "Distribution of ASD-STE100". The FAQ says the same and adds PDF is the only format.
- **Getting it:** the downloads page offers a request form for a "free official copy". The PDF URL above also downloaded with no form on 2026-10-28.
- **Redistribution:** unauthorized distribution is prohibited without written STEMG permission (same section). Do not commit the PDF or the dictionary to this repo.
- **Copyright and trademark:** ASD owns the copyright. "ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English" is an EU registered trademark (Issue 9, copyright page).
- **Notice on reuse:** the downloads page says STEMG and ASD issue no individual permissions, grant no right to use the trademark or logo, and do not endorse, certify or validate any third-party tool, including AI-based ones. Use of the content is at the user's own risk.
- **Special usage rights:** the copyright page names eight groups with irrevocable free reproduction rights (ASD members, AIA members, airlines, universities for education, and others). A software documentation team is not named. This matters only if rule or dictionary text is copied into the repo. Reading and applying the rules is free.
- **Claim wording:** the notice says no tool may be presented as approved by, affiliated with, or developed with STEMG or ASD. The docs should say "follows these rules from ASD-STE100 Issue 9", not "STE compliant" or "certified". This is an inference from the notice about tools and the trademark, not a statement about prose claims. Unverified legal reading.

## Comparison with `.agents/writing-language.md`

Legend. Conflict: the repo rule and STE give different requirements for the same thing, and following the repo rule can break STE. Stricter: the repo rule is tighter than STE, so following it also satisfies STE. Gap: STE has a rule the repo lacks. Extra: the repo has a rule STE does not address.

### Conflicts

| Repo rule | Repo says | STE says |
| --- | --- | --- |
| L3 Sentence length | Target 25 words or fewer, hard cap 40 | Procedure sentence max 20 (5.1). Descriptive sentence max 25 (6.3). Note sentence max 25 (5.5) |
| Contractions ("Not in this checklist", decision pending) | Undecided | Not allowed (4.2) |

The L3 cap of 40 breaks STE in both modes. The L3 target of 25 breaks STE for procedures. The quickstart is mostly procedure, so 20 is the figure that applies there. The repo has no procedure versus description split. L3 also does not say how to count words. STE counts parentheses, numbers, quoted text, labels and hyphenated words as one word each (8.5 to 8.7), so a manual count and a checker count can differ.

### Stricter than STE (not violations)

- **L4 Paragraph length:** repo 4 sentences or fewer, STE 6 (6.6).
- **L6 Active voice:** repo has no exception. STE allows the passive in descriptive text when the agent is unknown (3.6).
- **L7 No noun stacks:** repo says three nouns in a row need a verb. STE allows three words and bans four or more (2.1). "Connector-event handler wiring" is STE-legal under hyphen counting (8.7).
- **L1 Plain word:** STE enforces this with the approved dictionary, which is stricter than "plain".

### Compatible

- **L2 One idea per sentence:** matches 4.1, 5.2, 6.1.
- **L8 One name per thing:** matches 1.11 and 9.4.
- **V2 Imperative for steps:** matches 5.3.
- **T3 Informal words:** close to 1.10.
- **T1 Define Vector and SDK terms:** compatible with 1.5 and 1.8.

### Gaps (STE rules the repo lacks)

- Dictionary: words, part of speech, meaning (1.1 to 1.4, 9.2). The repo has no controlled vocabulary.
- No technical noun as a verb and no technical verb as a noun (1.7, 1.13). Developer words such as "log", "cache" and "query" are often used both ways.
- Allowed tenses (3.2, 3.4): no perfect or progressive forms, no "can be adjusted" constructions.
- "-ing" forms (3.5).
- Articles and no omitted words (4.2, 4.5).
- Condition first, then comma, then command (5.4).
- One instruction per sentence unless simultaneous (5.2).
- Notes: information only, no imperative, 25 words (5.5).
- No imperative in descriptive text (Section 6 intro). Repo V1 and V2 push toward "you" and imperative everywhere.
- No semicolons (8.1).
- Vertical-list punctuation (4.3).
- Phrasal verbs (9.3).
- Safety-instruction structure (7.1 to 7.3). Relevant for destructive commands if the quickstart has any.
- American spelling (1.14).

### Extras (repo rules outside STE)

- **L5 table cell 15 words:** STE is silent on tables.
- **L9 spell out figures:** STE has no unit rules (General introduction).
- **V1, V3, V4, V5** (address as "you", never name reader by role, role names, no "we"): STE is silent.
- **A1 to A5 abbreviations:** STE has no abbreviation rules (General introduction). These are house rules. STE counts an abbreviation as one word (8.6).
- **H1, H2 headings:** STE has no formatting rules.
- **C1 to C4 concept page structure:** no STE counterpart.

## Inputs for the quickstart decision

- Procedure text: the STE limit is 20 words, not 25. Only the Section 5 rules apply to steps. Notes and descriptive text use Section 6 (25 words, 6 sentences, no imperative).
- A full STE claim needs the 875-word dictionary plus a glossary of technical nouns. Applying selected writing rules does not.
- Wording of the claim: "follows these rules from ASD-STE100 Issue 9", with the rule list, and no compliance or certification claim.
- No checker can be recommended from a primary source.
